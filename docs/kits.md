# Kits from stocked components

In Stock, create a SKU for the kit and SKUs for its stocked components. Open the kit's item details, expand **Kits and assembly**, and define each component's exact quantity in its saved base unit. Choose whether a shortage backorders complete kits or blocks the order. A new definition is a new version; existing quotes and orders retain their saved definition. Component units cannot change after a definition references them.

A kit uses normal customer prices, item barcodes and channel SKU matching. A reserved quote is entered from Sales using **Document type → Reserved quote**; accept it before dispatch. Canceling remaining demand releases its reservations. An order reserves complete component sets at one warehouse, using compatible pre-assembled stock first. A shortage can leave whole kits backordered, with no partial component set. Component reservations count against normal stock and purchasing demand. A virtual kit without pre-assembled stock is not an independent replenishment item.

Availability is the sum of compatible available pre-assembled kits and the lowest whole buildable count across the components in that warehouse. The channel's buffer is deducted from each component's available base units, and from available pre-assembled kit units, before computing whole counts. Protected holds and other reservations remain unavailable. Recalculation follows every committed stock or reservation change.

Pick lists show the kit's component picks and bins. Scan those physical components into cartons; dispatch requires complete sets and consumes each reservation once. Sales links to a printable quote or packing slip with the saved definition, component quantities and reserved or dispatched bins. Operational shipment COGS sums the actual issued component layers; Reports also shows net kit margin after physical kit returns. Financial tax and credit-note reconciliation remains in Books.

To pre-assemble stock, open the kit's details, prepare a kitting task with the kit bin, component bins and quantity, review and approve it, then confirm the physical work. Assembly consumes unreserved components and receives kits at their rolled-up cost. Disassembly uses the assembled definition and returns its retained component quantities and costs. A different composition cannot satisfy an order for a new definition. All component and kit bins must be in one warehouse; existing permitted-item, dimension and capacity policies apply to the resulting stock. A changed or revoked approver cannot authorize posting.

For returns, select the actual kit dispatch in the item's details. Record every component's condition and inspection reason before receiving the kit into quarantine. A kit with any damaged component cannot be restocked as sellable. The existing Sales quarantine review controls restocking; a reviewed disassembly can recover an intact returned kit's components.

The full business JSON export retains definition versions, order snapshots, kitting tasks, component inspections and stock movements. The native CSV cutover shape cannot preserve open kit definitions and component allocations and refuses that export with an explanation. Use the complete installation archive and business export for those records.
