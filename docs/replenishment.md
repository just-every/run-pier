# Replenishment and customer dates

Open Purchasing → Demand and replenishment → Review policy. The 30, 90, 365 and chosen-day demand rates combine native shipments and mapped native channel orders, linked to their source documents. Channel demand uses first Pier capture time, is net of native cancellations and is assigned to the routed warehouse or the connection warehouse when no route is set. Linked shipments never add the same demand twice. This is captured demand, not a provider historical sales backfill. Lead-time demand plus your safety stock proposes a reorder point; the review period proposes a quantity. Save the supplier, lead time, safety stock and windows, then apply the proposed points and quantities. Applying them requires a buyer and current inputs. New sales never silently change your applied parameters.

A fixed-quantity rule adds its quantity and point, then subtracts the position. An order-up-to rule subtracts the position from its maximum. The position includes sellable stock minus reserved and backordered demand, plus committed purchase remainders and inbound transfers. Quarantine and forecasts do not count. Suggestions round to supplier packs and minimum quantities. Existing drafts reduce the remaining suggestion, so repeated planning does not buy the same shortage twice. The list shows each calculation term, expected freight/duty and landed cost. Automatic drafts prepare purchases within the saved item/warehouse policy; a buyer still approves each exact PO. Turning off automatic drafts pauses that policy.

Review the PO, approve and commit its exact version, then review the recipient under Send purchase order to supplier. The approval covers the PO contents, expected cost and at most five confirmation chases spaced 1–30 days apart. Every minute the scheduler checks due approved messages and low-stock policies. Chases require provider acceptance of the initial send and stop when confirmed, fully received, materially changed or paused. Use Stop supplier chases to revoke them. Read approved messages and delivery outcomes on the record. A provider acceptance means submitted; it does not establish inbox delivery. Failed or uncertain submissions are not retried blindly. Inspect the provider before authorizing a new message.

To delegate the initial send, commit the buyer-approved PO and open **Let the inventory agent send this PO**. Approve its exact recipient, landed USD spend ceiling, expiry and reminder bounds. The scheduled inventory agent sends it once; an authenticated agent can also run `po.sendApproved` with only the PO identity/version. It cannot choose another recipient, change the PO, approve a purchase or exceed the ceiling. **Stop delegated send and chases** revokes pending work. Changes to demand, supplier terms, replenishment policy or business destination invalidate the authority and require renewed buyer review. Expiry stops an initial send; already authorized reminder intervals stay bounded. The record retains the approving human, executor, message and provider outcome. An unavailable provider may be configured later, but failed or uncertain submissions require reconciliation and are never blindly retried.

Record the supplier’s actual reply and message reference on the PO. Approving a confirmed date keeps that reply, updates the PO and recomputes affected customer dates. Incoming mail is recorded by an authorized person in this round; mailbox polling and automatic extraction are not implemented.

Sales orders show backordered quantities, the POs supplying them and whether their date is confirmed or an estimate. Supply quantities are consumed once in customer-promise/order-date priority. Without enough confirmed supply, a supplier lead-time estimate is clearly labeled. Review the computed date and customer recipient to send a re-promise, or authorize Future date updates for that exact order and recipient. Later confirmed PO date changes re-promise and email automatically within that authorization. Disable the policy to stop future automatic updates. Customer promise history and every approved message remain visible.

Business email uses the install’s configured `MAIL_PROVIDER` (`resend`, `sendgrid` or `cloudflare`), `MAIL_FROM`, `MAIL_API_KEY` and, for Cloudflare, `MAIL_ACCOUNT_ID`. Authentication keeps its existing local sign-in behavior. Missing business mail configuration and RER Managed installations show one line: **Email is not available yet.** No provider call is attempted on Managed. Fix configuration and use Process due approved emails to submit previously unavailable messages; ambiguous submissions remain unresolved. Restored installs do not acquire approval authority from retained identities: establish fresh native access, review policies and reconnect deliberately.

Forecasts use native shipments and captured native channel demand. Incumbent history and historical channel backfill remain separate source boundaries. The command comparison described below adds reviewed forecast scenarios and retained receipt follow-through; it does not establish a service guarantee or a global purchasing optimum. Dates and forecasts do not create stock or guarantee a physical delivery.

## Slow-mover warnings

Reports → Slow-mover warnings lets a buyer approve an email destination, a warning period (90 days by default), a later dead-stock period (180 days), and quiet hours in UTC. The minute scheduler flags owned SKU/bin stock before it reaches the dead-stock threshold, assigning a buyer, due date and next action. The warning shows quantity, exact or unknown USD value, inactivity start, last movement and days remaining. An inactivity episode starts when stock first arrives and resets on a sale shipment or transfer out; an additional receipt, count or status change does not erase the clock. New inventory is not immediately dead stock.

One exception and one notification are retained per inactivity episode. A sale or transfer closes the warning with its movement; a buyer can close it with a documented decision. Repeated checks keep the same warning and do not repeat its email. A buyer may also **Flag stock for early review** with an actual reason, such as an assortment change; it uses the same approved notification and closure path. Pausing or changing the policy revokes pending notifications. Quiet hours delay mail; failed or uncertain provider submissions remain visible without blind retries. At most 25 new exceptions and ten new notifications are prepared per scheduled check; remaining work continues at later checks.

The report's default dead-stock threshold follows the saved policy; report filters can still select another threshold explicitly. Calculations use native Pier movements and UTC day cutoffs. Imported incumbent sales, kit consumption, general escalation, Slack/WhatsApp and Managed email remain separate contract gaps. An email is a request for buyer work; it does not dispose of stock or close a warning.

## Workflow draft provenance

Owner workflow jobs retain the exact enabling rule snapshot. Each generated purchase retains its rule identity, revision, job and draft-only mark. An edit pauses the rule and immediately marks queued work failed with a visible human-review reason. A fresh duplicate command cannot overwrite a resolved outcome or release its consumed daily budget. The workflow grants no approval or supplier send; a buyer separately authorizes the exact committed purchase. Changing the governing workflow or planning scope revokes that delegated send and requires renewed review.

## Compare cash and service plans

In Purchasing, choose **Review policy**, then **Compare cash and service plans**. Enter the cash ceiling and optionally review a transfer source bin, destination bin, transit location, arrival date, transport estimate and its source. The rankings show quantity, shortage, arrival, cash and permitted contribution evidence. Choosing a purchase creates a draft; its approval and supplier send remain separate. An owner can dispatch a selected transfer through the existing warehouse command. Changed inputs require a fresh comparison.

A buyer or agent can submit `replenishment.compare` to the authenticated command door with `itemId`, `warehouse`, optional `supplierId`, and a decimal USD `cashCeiling`. The response includes its `stateVersion` and four options: minimum covers supplier lead time, recommended adds one review period, stretch adds two, and cash-constrained caps the recommended quantity. All net the current supply position and existing drafts, apply the supplier minimum and case packs, and include configured freight and duty. Unaffordable or noncompliant options explain why they cannot be executed. This comparison changes no stock, purchase, approval or outbound message.

A buyer selects an executable quantity through `replenishment.plan`, passing the comparison's `stateVersion` and a `lines` array containing the exact item, warehouse, supplier and quantity. Any intervening command requires a fresh comparison. The cash ceiling is an input for comparing this proposed line, not a grant of spending authority or a persistent company budget. Purchase approval and send authority remain separate. The original four horizon options retain their observed-rate basis. The additional `optimization` result uses the forecast scenario described below. The two rankings compare currently executable purchases and any reviewed transfers. They are bounded single-item scenarios, not a portfolio optimum or a service probability.


## Reviewed forecast scenarios and actuals

Open **Forecast settings** in the policy editor to save reviewed low, expected and high seasonal and promotion factors and their source notes. These settings are separate from choosing a purchase or dispatching a transfer.

`replenishment.save` accepts optional `forecastAssumptions` with `seasonality` and `promotion`. Each carries positive ordered `low`, `expected` and `high` decimal factors (at most ten) and a nonempty `source` note. A current buyer reviews them with the ordinary policy version. Omission retains the saved assumptions; explicit `null` removes the saved override, after which an applicable SKU default may still supply assumptions. These are reviewed scenario inputs, not automatically learned effects or a grant of spending authority. Any policy edit invalidates pending delegated purchase sends.

`replenishment.compare` returns an `optimization` object. It reconstructs physical in-stock milliseconds over the selected window from sellable movements in the demand warehouse. Where the full window and positive exposure exist, observed captured demand divided by the in-stock fraction gives an availability-normalized scenario. A new item, an incomplete window or a wholly unavailable window reports insufficient exposure rather than inventing lost sales. Captured orders may include backorders, and actual lost sales remain unobservable: the correction is an assumption, not a measured demand total. The bounds include observed demand and the reviewed seasonal and promotion factors; they are not a statistical confidence interval.

The retained-current-demand backtest splits the window in two: only captures at or before the split train an observed-rate baseline, then the response reports the later actual quantity and absolute error. Current cancellations affect retained net demand; this is not a replay of historical order revisions or a validation of subsequently entered seasonal factors.

Purchase alternatives cover every current supplier term for the item, retaining MOQ, case pack, freight, duty and drafts. Completed and overdue commitments supply observed delay evidence; missing supplier history is shown as unknown. For the current owner, cash ranking uses realized native item contribution per proposed landed dollar where priced and valued shipments exist, followed by shortage, delay and cost. Service ranking covers the upper scenario through two review periods and compares remaining shortage, delay and cost. This is a single-item heuristic, not a portfolio optimizer. Other roles receive no revenue or realized margin and fall back to the disclosed shortage, delay and cost order. Authorized native invoice item prices and allocated discounts supersede order prices; authorized item credits reduce revenue. Physical returns reserve their net item value until credited, without subtracting that value twice, and only sellable restocked cost is recovered. A prepay return credit belongs to its physical shipment rather than being spread across the order’s other shipments. Draft invoices and credits do not change actual contribution. Item losses remain negative in the evidence and ranked alternatives; separately charged freight cannot floor them to zero. Tax, freight income and financing are outside item contribution. Unpriced, unvalued, kit and unconverted non-USD lines are excluded rather than valued by assumption. The response includes an exact `replenishment.plan` payload and state version. A later command makes that version stale; compare again before drafting. Approval and sending still use the existing exact human doors.

Transfer comparisons show surplus after source open demand, outbound commitments and safety stock. Supply `transferQuotes` to `replenishment.compare`, with `fromLocationId`, `toLocationId`, `transitLocationId`, `expectedDate`, `transportCost` and a `reason` identifying the estimate source. Each reviewed transfer competes in the cash and service rankings only when stock and its arrival fit the objective; the cash ranking also enforces the entered ceiling. Transport cash is incremental; retained source stock cost remains part of expected contribution. No immediate arrival or free transport is inferred. The exact `transfer.send` consequence retains the quote and comparison state version, checks source protection again and requires current warehouse authority. Comparisons move no stock. Dispatch is an explicit physical action, replay posts no second movement, and arrival still uses the ordinary transfer receipt door. A transport estimate is not a paid carrier bill or a new cost layer.

Generated replenishment drafts retain the exact forecast assumptions, bounds, backtest and demand identities. `replenishment.review` takes `poId` and returns current `followThrough`: retained low, expected and high forecasts versus later captured demand, absolute error, whether actuals fall inside reviewed bounds, posted received quantities, expected versus actual landed totals including late bills, receipt obligation states and inventory reconciliation. It remains open until all goods arrive, receipt obligations are paid and the inventory ledger reconciles. Reversal or lost accounting connection reopens the evidence. It reports the ledger check time; it does not make a new provider reconciliation. This command is available to current buyers and agents and grants no stock, approval or outbound authority.

Open the selected purchase and choose **Review current receipt and ledger** under **Forecast versus actual**. The screen shows remaining goods, expected and actual landed totals, receipt obligation states and current inventory reconciliation. It reports an open outcome until physical and financial closure is supported; new records require another review. The later actual comparison validates the retained reviewed scenario prospectively, alongside the rolling-origin baseline backtest. Neither rewrites the original forecast.

Delegated supplier sends retain their computed money and external classes, exact landed spend including supplier tax, terms, recipient, policy revision, approving human and executor. Execution rechecks the current named approval route, including delegation expiry, before queuing a message. This is the existing exact-human native send policy; it does not certify the separately tracked MCP conformance surface.

## Compare an exact substitute

An owner opens Activity → Settings → Substitute settings to record an exact same-base-unit alternative and its reason. Only active untracked SKUs outside kits are supported. Record a manual supplier confirmation on the approved committed purchase first, then open **Compare supplier substitutes** on Activity. Pier finds the delayed confirmed supply linked to current customer shortages and prepares a retained comparison. An authenticated native agent can prepare the same comparison through the public command door.

Each affected line shows its customer promise, supplier date, proposed quantity and uncovered demand. Bin evidence separates physical stock, reservations belonging to every order and protected holds. Eligible availability is consumed once across the proposed lines; missing mappings remain uncovered rather than inventing an alternative. Expand **Exact source evidence** to inspect record identities, versions and content digests in the owner export.

Review accepts or declines the recommendation only. It changes no stock, reservation, order, purchase, customer promise or message. If an input or current authority changes, prepare again before reviewing. Previous decisions remain historical evidence after later changes and restart. This native comparison provides no mailbox connection, model-derived equivalence, executable substitution or sending authority.
