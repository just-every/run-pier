# Replenishment and customer dates

Open Purchasing → Demand and replenishment → Review policy. The 30, 90, 365 and chosen-day demand rates combine native shipments and mapped native channel orders, linked to their source documents. Channel demand uses first Pier capture time, is net of native cancellations and is assigned to the routed warehouse or the connection warehouse when no route is set. Linked shipments never add the same demand twice. This is captured demand, not a provider historical sales backfill. Lead-time demand plus your safety stock proposes a reorder point; the review period proposes a quantity. Save the supplier, lead time, safety stock and windows, then apply the proposed points and quantities. Applying them requires a buyer and current inputs. New sales never silently change your applied parameters.

A fixed-quantity rule adds its quantity and point, then subtracts the position. An order-up-to rule subtracts the position from its maximum. The position includes sellable stock minus reserved and backordered demand, plus committed purchase remainders and inbound transfers. Quarantine and forecasts do not count. Suggestions round to supplier packs and minimum quantities. Existing drafts reduce the remaining suggestion, so repeated planning does not buy the same shortage twice. The list shows each calculation term, expected freight/duty and landed cost. Automatic drafts prepare purchases within the saved item/warehouse policy; a buyer still approves each exact PO. Turning off automatic drafts pauses that policy.

Review the PO, approve and commit its exact version, then review the recipient under Send purchase order to supplier. The approval covers the PO contents, expected cost and at most five confirmation chases spaced 1–30 days apart. Every minute the scheduler checks due approved messages and low-stock policies. Chases require provider acceptance of the initial send and stop when confirmed, fully received, materially changed or paused. Use Stop supplier chases to revoke them. Read approved messages and delivery outcomes on the record. A provider acceptance means submitted; it does not establish inbox delivery. Failed or uncertain submissions are not retried blindly. Inspect the provider before authorizing a new message.

To delegate the initial send, commit the buyer-approved PO and open **Let the inventory agent send this PO**. Approve its exact recipient, landed USD spend ceiling, expiry and reminder bounds. The scheduled inventory agent sends it once; an authenticated agent can also run `po.sendApproved` with only the PO identity/version. It cannot choose another recipient, change the PO, approve a purchase or exceed the ceiling. **Stop delegated send and chases** revokes pending work. Changes to demand, supplier terms, replenishment policy or business destination invalidate the authority and require renewed buyer review. Expiry stops an initial send; already authorized reminder intervals stay bounded. The record retains the approving human, executor, message and provider outcome. An unavailable provider may be configured later, but failed or uncertain submissions require reconciliation and are never blindly retried.

Record the supplier’s actual reply and message reference on the PO. Approving a confirmed date keeps that reply, updates the PO and recomputes affected customer dates. Incoming mail is recorded by an authorized person in this round; mailbox polling and automatic extraction are not implemented.

Sales orders show backordered quantities, the POs supplying them and whether their date is confirmed or an estimate. Supply quantities are consumed once in customer-promise/order-date priority. Without enough confirmed supply, a supplier lead-time estimate is clearly labeled. Review the computed date and customer recipient to send a re-promise, or authorize Future date updates for that exact order and recipient. Later confirmed PO date changes re-promise and email automatically within that authorization. Disable the policy to stop future automatic updates. Customer promise history and every approved message remain visible.

Business email uses the install’s configured `MAIL_PROVIDER` (`resend`, `sendgrid` or `cloudflare`), `MAIL_FROM`, `MAIL_API_KEY` and, for Cloudflare, `MAIL_ACCOUNT_ID`. Authentication keeps its existing local sign-in behavior. Missing business mail configuration and RER Managed installations show one line: **Email is not available yet.** No provider call is attempted on Managed. Fix configuration and use Process due approved emails to submit previously unavailable messages; ambiguous submissions remain unresolved. Restored installs do not acquire approval authority from retained identities: establish fresh native access, review policies and reconnect deliberately.

Forecasts use native shipments and captured native channel demand. Incumbent history, historical channel backfill, seasonality, promotions, stockout correction, service-level optimization, cash limits and full forecast-versus-actual accounting closure remain named target gaps in CONTRACT.md. Dates and forecasts do not create stock or guarantee a physical delivery.

## Slow-mover warnings

Reports → Slow-mover warnings lets a buyer approve an email destination, a warning period (90 days by default), a later dead-stock period (180 days), and quiet hours in UTC. The minute scheduler flags owned SKU/bin stock before it reaches the dead-stock threshold, assigning a buyer, due date and next action. The warning shows quantity, exact or unknown USD value, inactivity start, last movement and days remaining. An inactivity episode starts when stock first arrives and resets on a sale shipment or transfer out; an additional receipt, count or status change does not erase the clock. New inventory is not immediately dead stock.

One exception and one notification are retained per inactivity episode. A sale or transfer closes the warning with its movement; a buyer can close it with a documented decision. Repeated checks keep the same warning and do not repeat its email. A buyer may also **Flag stock for early review** with an actual reason, such as an assortment change; it uses the same approved notification and closure path. Pausing or changing the policy revokes pending notifications. Quiet hours delay mail; failed or uncertain provider submissions remain visible without blind retries. At most 25 new exceptions and ten new notifications are prepared per scheduled check; remaining work continues at later checks.

The report's default dead-stock threshold follows the saved policy; report filters can still select another threshold explicitly. Calculations use native Pier movements and UTC day cutoffs. Imported incumbent sales, kit consumption, general escalation, Slack/WhatsApp and Managed email remain separate contract gaps. An email is a request for buyer work; it does not dispose of stock or close a warning.

## Workflow draft provenance

Owner workflow jobs retain the exact enabling rule snapshot. Each generated purchase retains its rule identity, revision, job and draft-only mark. An edit pauses the rule and immediately marks queued work failed with a visible human-review reason. A fresh duplicate command cannot overwrite a resolved outcome or release its consumed daily budget. The workflow grants no approval or supplier send; a buyer separately authorizes the exact committed purchase. Changing the governing workflow or planning scope revokes that delegated send and requires renewed review.

## Compare bounded purchases through the command API

A buyer or agent can submit `replenishment.compare` to the authenticated command door with `itemId`, `warehouse`, optional `supplierId`, and a decimal USD `cashCeiling`. The response includes its `stateVersion` and four options: minimum covers supplier lead time, recommended adds one review period, stretch adds two, and cash-constrained caps the recommended quantity. All net the current supply position and existing drafts, apply the supplier minimum and case packs, and include configured freight and duty. Unaffordable or noncompliant options explain why they cannot be executed. This comparison changes no stock, purchase, approval or outbound message.

A buyer selects an executable quantity through `replenishment.plan`, passing the comparison's `stateVersion` and a `lines` array containing the exact item, warehouse, supplier and quantity. Any intervening command requires a fresh comparison. The cash ceiling is an input for comparing this proposed line, not a grant of spending authority or a persistent company budget. Purchase approval and send authority remain separate. These horizon calculations are estimates at the observed rate; seasonal forecasts, stockout correction, service probabilities, transfer alternatives and an interactive comparison screen remain unfinished.
