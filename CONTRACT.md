# Pier product contract

Target edition: **16 October 2026**, revised 17 October 2026 after buyer review rounds 1, 2 and 3 and the homepage claims review. US first buyer: the owner or operations manager of a small finished-goods wholesaler or distributor, operating in **USD**, usually keeping QuickBooks Online.

Pier is a connected inventory and order management system: know what can be promised, replenish in time, execute warehouse work accurately and carry every order through shipment and reconciled accounting. A buyer switching from **Cin7 Core, inFlow Inventory or Fishbowl Warehouse** must be able to run that whole job. Planning and automation extend beyond the inventory work represented by **Prediko and DOSS**, with actual operational closure rather than forecasts and alerts alone.

This is the product we are building toward, not a description of everything available today. The named clauses are requirements; their acceptance journeys are targets until supported by the status table and [ACCEPTANCE.md](ACCEPTANCE.md). The inspected code is `fe37107650b9a328b4e92945784039f255086b97`, the same commit as the 16 October inventory code audit, re-judged at `1c0f1c674f4227a2ed79476be5e3ef3d29f7b92c` on 17 October 2026 (see [Status](#status)). `done` means the stated current local behavior has implementation and exercising tests; it does not establish real provider, hosted, legal or physical-device acceptance. `partial` states the implemented subset; `missing` has no implementation of the promised behavior. Future journeys do not become evidence by being written here. Every capability claim on the approved homepage maps to the clauses that promise it in [Homepage claims](#homepage-claims).

## Product separation and US operating boundary

Pier owns inventory, wholesale commercial rules, purchasing, warehouse execution, fulfillment and operational reconciliation. [Stall](https://github.com/just-every/run-stall) owns the Shopify-style public/private storefront, cart and checkout experience; Pier is its stock system, including the private trade-ordering connection in SALES-005. [Larder](https://github.com/just-every/run-larder) owns manufacturing/MRP, manufacturing BOMs, production orders and shop-floor work; Pier exchanges finished-goods demand, availability and receipts through reviewed integration. Kits and bundles assembled from stocked finished goods without transforming them (case assortments, display kits, gift sets) are inventory work that Pier owns under KIT-001, as Cin7 Core and inFlow treat them. Larder owns BOMs that transform materials through operations, routings, work in progress, scrap and yield, and a Larder-built SKU arrives in Pier as a reviewed finished-goods receipt. No manufacturing scope is absorbed from the older research’s Katana/production examples. Neither boundary cuts the connected inventory job.

US norms include USD operational valuation, US addresses and time zones, sales-tax/exemption evidence, ACH where used for payments, and explicit accounting period controls. Foreign-currency supplier purchases retain their source value and reviewed USD conversion; they do not change the US first-buyer focus. Native CSV import defaults to USD and records the reviewed rate for other currencies (PIER-003).

Pier integrates carrier networks through ShipStation/EasyPost, EDI transport through SPS Commerce/Orderful and third-party warehouses through ShipHero/Extensiv; it does not operate those networks. QuickBooks Online/Xero remain the general ledger and accountant-approved books; Square/Stripe handle payment processing, and tax filing remains with the accountant/tax provider. Pier owns operational valuation, document exchange, reconciliation and unresolved exceptions, including partner onboarding and credentials required to make those workflows work. Ordinary lot traceability is included; regulated release/recall certification, customs brokerage and bonded-warehouse compliance use specialist partners with retained evidence. Background forecasting and document processing use bounded jobs/approved compute rather than unbounded request handlers.

“Pier” remains a working product name, not a legal name-clearance assertion. Hosted, provider, security-baseline, device/printer, owner-export, accessibility and physical-stock acceptance each require their own evidence; their difficulty does not remove a target clause. Existing owner-approved homepage, interface and behavior are unchanged by this document round.

## Existing twelve operator journeys

The original journey numbers 1–12 are retained in order as PIER-001–PIER-012. Their established native behavior remains part of the full product, with broader capabilities specified below. Existing local test evidence is preserved rather than relabeled as proof of new integrations.

### PIER-001 — Define stock.

An operator creates a SKU, unique barcode, base unit, exact pack conversion and warehouse/bin locations, with sellable and quarantine stock. Archiving a used SKU preserves its history. Once movements, counts, supplier terms, orders or purchase orders reference an item, its unit and pack conversion are immutable; a location’s bin/transit kind is immutable after movements or counts reference it.

**Acceptance evidence:** Journey 1: create fractional-pack stock and bin/transit locations through the UI and authenticated commands, reference them with counts and documents, then attempt unit/pack/kind edits and archive the item. Edits fail atomically and archived history remains readable.

### PIER-002 — Maintain counterparties and supplier terms.

Operators link suppliers and customers. Each supplier/item pair has one versioned terms record with supplier code, pack, price, minimum order and lead time. Purchase commitments and receipts retain their exact terms snapshot when the supplier later changes terms.

**Acceptance evidence:** Journey 2: create supplier terms, commit a PO, revise terms and read the original PO/receipt. The commitment retains its original quantities, price and source version; duplicate-pair creation cannot bypass the versioned record.

### PIER-003 — Review native opening inventory.

Owners stage native CSV items, locations, counterparties, opening positions and open documents; review mappings, originals and reconciliation; resolve ambiguous rows; and approve one cutover. Optional reservations preserve exact per-line/bin allocations, including explicit zero backorders. Without that file, the six-kind importer reserves available stock. Changed source rows require reviewed revision, and downstream dependencies constrain undo. Safe undo before downstream work restores exact before-images and removes only that import’s created graph/movements; retained immutable originals and import custody explain this narrow exception to append-only operational corrections. Native CSV openings, open documents and counterparty prices carry an ISO 4217 currency per file or row. USD is the default for a US install: a row with no currency is read as USD and the preview shows it as USD, and an unknown currency code is refused with the row named. A non-USD opening, for example an AUD or CAD supplier cost as Cin7 Core, inFlow and Fishbowl allow, keeps its source amount and currency and is valued in USD only through a conversion rate the owner reviews and the batch records, never a silent or guessed rate, consistent with the foreign-currency rule in the US norms above and COST-001.

**Acceptance evidence:** Journey 3: preview and freeze a native batch, commit once, repeat unchanged input, revise a changed opening and exercise safe versus dependency-blocked undo. Import a wholly backordered and a bin-allocated line with reservations.csv; no duplicate opening or silent reallocation appears. Import a USD native opening batch and read USD unit costs and valuation in COST-001’s ledger. Import a mixed batch with AUD rows and review its USD conversion rate before commit; the committed batch records that rate beside the source amounts and currency. A row with no currency on a US install is read as USD and shown so in the preview. An unknown currency code is refused with the row named.

### PIER-004 — Commit sales demand without overselling.

Sales staff enter customer order lines. Pier reserves sellable availability atomically and exposes shortages as backorders. It never creates stock, reserves the same unit twice or permits negative sellable stock. Quarantine, in-transit and expected receipts cannot satisfy a present-stock reservation.

**Acceptance evidence:** Journey 4: submit simultaneous orders for the last fractional units through authenticated HTTP and retry a lost response with the same command. Total reservations never exceed on-hand sellable stock and unmet demand remains visible.

### PIER-005 — Approve exact purchase commitments.

A buyer reviews shortages and expected receipts, drafts a supplier PO, approves its exact supplier, quantity, price and promise date, then commits it. Material changes invalidate approval. Ordered quantities remain expected until a real receipt posts; an agent may prepare evidence but cannot approve its own purchase.

**Acceptance evidence:** Journey 5: prepare a PO as an agent, attempt self-approval, obtain buyer approval, change a material term and attempt commit. Only a freshly approved version commits and it changes expected supply rather than on-hand stock.

### PIER-006 — Receive partial purchases atomically.

Warehouse staff receive some or all of a committed purchase into named bins, recording supplier reference and exact receipt cost. The remainder stays expected. Invalid or over-received lines cannot partially post an otherwise valid multi-line receipt.

**Acceptance evidence:** Journey 6: partially receive a multi-line PO, repeat the command, then submit one invalid line. The first receipt posts once, exact cost is retained, remaining expected quantities reconcile and the invalid receipt leaves every line unchanged.

### PIER-007 — Correct receipts with physical evidence.

Warehouse staff can reverse a receipt only when later work does not depend on it. With downstream movements, they capture and approve a physical count for a receipt bin; receipt.correct posts a linked compensating adjustment at that frontier. Originals remain and a stale count cannot overwrite later movement.

**Acceptance evidence:** Journey 7: reverse an unused receipt, attempt reversal after stock is reserved or dispatched, then correct against an approved physical count. Intervening movement invalidates the old count; successful correction retains the original and its compensating link.

### PIER-008 — Transfer through visible transit.

Warehouse staff dispatch available units through a real transit location and separately confirm destination receipt. Transit stock is unavailable to sales and transfers conserve quantity. Repeated commands cannot dispatch or receive twice.

**Acceptance evidence:** Journey 8: dispatch a transfer, try to sell its in-transit units, partially receive at the destination and retry. Source plus transit plus destination reconciles throughout and transit cannot be promised as current sellable stock.

### PIER-009 — Dispatch only owned reservations.

Warehouse staff partially dispatch an order against that order’s own reservations. Shipment identity links order lines and consumed reservations; undispatched demand remains outstanding. Another order’s allocation cannot be consumed and a retry never decrements stock twice.

**Acceptance evidence:** Journey 9: partially dispatch an order, attempt to use a foreign reservation and retry after response loss. Read the shipment and remaining order: exact consumed allocations and outstanding demand reconcile.

### PIER-010 — Cancel and inspect returns.

Staff cancel only unshipped demand and release only its remaining reservations. A return points to an actual dispatch line and cannot exceed dispatched units less prior returns. It enters quarantine and requires separately reviewed restock before becoming sellable.

**Acceptance evidence:** Journey 10: cancel the remainder of a partially shipped order, return shipped units, reject an excess return and review a partial restock. Shipment history survives and quarantined units cannot be sold before restock.

### PIER-011 — Count at a captured movement frontier.

Warehouse staff capture a movement frontier, record a physical count, inspect variance and approve an exact adjustment or recount. A movement after capture makes the review stale; a model estimate cannot replace a physical count.

**Acceptance evidence:** Journey 11: capture and count a bin, move stock, then attempt the old approval. It fails without changing stock; a new captured count can produce the exact approved compensating movement.

### PIER-012 — Investigate and leave with native data.

People trace balances to movements, source documents, actors, reasons, command identities and reversal/import provenance. They export business records and costs and restore the complete current installation archive into a fresh local installation without posting openings twice. Durable operator and approval identities are historical evidence rather than access grants; operation requires a fresh native session.

**Acceptance evidence:** Journey 12: investigate a corrected receipt and dispatch, export, restore into fresh local D1, compare business records and journal links, replay an unchanged import and execute a new authorized transaction after fresh sign-in.

## Target clauses: complete connected wholesale operations

### STOCK-001 — Stock position across warehouses and bins.

For every item, Pier shows its stock at each warehouse and bin: on hand (sellable), reserved, available (sellable less reservations), quarantine, in transit and expected (unreceived quantities on committed PO lines destined for that warehouse), with totals per warehouse and across the company. Figures are computed from the append-only movements, current reservations and open PO lines, never stored as editable balances, so each bin’s figure equals the sum of its movements (PIER-012). Staff search by SKU, barcode or name and filter by warehouse, bin and status on desktop and at 390px; an item with no positions says so rather than showing invented zeros per bin.

**Acceptance evidence:** Through public commands, define one item at two warehouses with two bins each, receive a partial PO into a bin at each, reserve an order, transfer units through transit, return units to quarantine and commit a second PO to the second warehouse. The Stock view and the state API show, per warehouse and bin, on hand, reserved, available, quarantine, transit and expected, each equal to an independent sum of the exported movements, reservations and open PO lines by destination; the warehouse and company totals add up; filters by warehouse, bin and status and search by SKU and barcode return exactly the matching rows at 1440px and 390px.

### CAT-001 — Wholesale catalog and units.

The catalog adds variant families, categories, supplier and customer item references, images, weights and dimensions, case-pack ordering and exact conversions to the stock definitions above. Bulk import/edit previews collisions and affected documents; used-unit invariants remain. Each SKU holds several barcodes (its own code, the manufacturer’s unit UPC/EAN, inner and case GTIN-14/ITF-14 codes, and supplier- or customer-specific codes), each bound to a unit of measure or pack quantity in base units; uniqueness is enforced across every barcode of every SKU and every bin, lot and shipment code, so a scan resolves to exactly one SKU and quantity (SCAN-002). Search and filters work across SKU, every barcode, aliases and archived history without hiding duplicate matches. Owners define typed custom fields on items, customers, suppliers and orders; those fields appear in filters, imports, exports and PRINT-001 documents.

**Acceptance evidence:** Import variants with supplier/customer aliases, dimensions and fractional packs through catalog import; preview a bulk change, give one SKU a unit UPC and a 12-unit case barcode, reject the same UPC on a second SKU (both owners named) and search by each alias and barcode. A case-pack quote, pick and label retain the same base-unit quantity. Add a custom field to items and orders, filter by it, print it on a packing slip and find it in export.

### KIT-001 — Kits and bundles from stocked components.

Owners define a kit SKU from stocked component SKUs with exact per-kit quantities and units, as Cin7 Core assembly kits and inFlow bundles do. A kit has its own price, barcode, documents and channel mapping but no independent physical stock unless pre-assembled. Selling a kit on a quote, sales order or channel order reserves every component atomically at one warehouse; a shortage of any component backorders or blocks the whole kit under the configured policy and never reserves a partial set. Published kit availability is the lowest whole-kit count buildable from each component’s sellable availability net of reservations and channel buffers, recalculated whenever a component moves. Pick lists and packing slips show the kit line with its component picks and bins; dispatch consumes each component reservation once; COGS is the sum of the component cost layers actually consumed, and margin is reported per kit. Owners may pre-assemble kits through a reviewed kitting task that consumes components and receives the kit SKU at a rolled-up cost, and disassemble them the same way. Kit returns are inspected per component. Kit definitions are versioned and an order keeps the version it reserved. Kitting changes no material, so it is inventory work in Pier, not Larder manufacturing.

**Acceptance evidence:** Define a two-component case-assortment kit and a three-component display kit from stocked SKUs. Sell the first on a sales order and through a connected channel concurrently: component reservations, published kit availability (the lowest buildable count across components), the pick list and COGS agree. Drain one component: kit availability drops to zero and a further kit order backorders as a whole kit. Pre-assemble five display kits, ship two, return one and inspect its components: rolled-up kit cost, component stock and kit margin reconcile, and no component is reserved twice.

### TRACE-001 — Serial, lot and expiry traceability.

Receiving, bin moves, allocations, picking, dispatch and returns retain serial/lot identity and expiry. Lot, serial and expiry can be entered by hand or filled from a scanned GS1 barcode (SCAN-002). Operators can quarantine a lot, choose FIFO/FEFO within approved policy and trace suppliers to affected customers and back. Duplicate serials, expired picks and wrong-lot scans are blocked or require an authorized recorded exception. Ordinary traceability does not certify regulated release or recall compliance.

**Acceptance evidence:** Receive two dated lots and serialized units through warehouse screens, transfer and split-dispatch them, quarantine one lot and return a serial. Trace the exact customers and receipts; a duplicate serial and expired/blocked lot cannot silently ship.

### SCAN-001 — Generate, scan and print warehouse barcodes.

Operators generate and validate item, bin, lot and shipment barcodes and print appropriate labels on Zebra and DYMO printers. USB/Bluetooth scanners, Zebra TC-series and comparable rugged Android handhelds whose built-in scanner works as a keyboard wedge, and iOS/Android cameras work through the warehouse workflow. Pier reads UPC-A, UPC-E, EAN-13, EAN-8, Code 128, GS1-128 (including SSCC (00) and GTIN-14 (01) with lot (10), expiry (17), production date (11), serial (21) and count (30/37) application identifiers), ITF-14, Code 39, QR Code and Data Matrix including GS1 DataMatrix. Pier prints Code 128 and GS1-128 (item, bin, lot and SSCC carton labels), QR Code (bin and task labels), UPC-A/EAN-13 for owner-assigned GTINs and ITF-14 case labels, on Zebra ZPL labels of 4×6 in (shipping and SSCC), 2×1 in and 2.25×1.25 in (item and bin), DYMO 30252 and 30334 labels, and Letter label sheets (Avery 5160 layout) through browser printing. Unknown or ambiguous scans ask for resolution; print failure never marks physical work complete. Supplier and case barcodes in receiving, picking and packing follow SCAN-002. Reprinting a carton or pallet label reproduces the same SSCC and contents rather than issuing a new serial, records who reprinted it and why on the shipment, and a reprint after the EDI-001 856 was sent changes no ASN data.

**Acceptance evidence:** At a warehouse device, print a real item/bin label, scan it by USB scanner, a Zebra TC-series keyboard-wedge handheld and phone camera and complete a receive-to-pick handoff. Scan a reference sheet carrying every named read symbology on each device type: each resolves to its encoded value (GS1 application identifiers parsed into fields). Print every named label size and symbology and scan the printed result back. Duplicate/unknown scans, a disconnected printer and a mismatched label produce actionable errors without stock effects; retain device/printer acceptance separately from software tests. Reprint the GS1-128 label of carton 7 of a shipment whose 856 was sent: the reprint carries the same SSCC, scans back to the same carton contents and appears on the shipment record with its reason, and the sent 856 is unchanged.

### SCAN-002 — Supplier unit, case and GS1 barcodes in warehouse work.

Receivers, pickers and packers scan whatever barcode is on the goods: Pier’s own labels, the manufacturer’s UPC-A/EAN-13 on each unit, the GTIN-14/ITF-14 case code on the carton or a GS1-128 SSCC on a retail-ready carton or pallet. Each barcode resolves through CAT-001 to one SKU and the unit or pack quantity it is bound to, always converted to base units. On a PO receipt line a unit barcode receives one unit; a case barcode receives the case quantity, and the received and remaining quantities convert (one 4-unit case scanned on a 10-unit line receives 4 and leaves 6). A case that would over-receive the line asks for an authorized over-receipt or a partial-case count rather than posting. A GS1-128 or GS1 DataMatrix carrying lot (10), expiry (17), production date (11) or serial (21) fills those TRACE-001 fields on the receipt from the barcode, and an expired or blocked lot is refused under TRACE-001. An inbound SSCC linked to an EDI-001 856 ASN or a supplier advance notice receives every line on that carton or pallet in one confirmed scan; outbound cartons get their own SSCC labels for EDI-001. At pick, a unit scan counts one unit and a case scan counts the case quantity against the pick line’s base-unit quantity; a different SKU, wrong lot or unknown code is refused with no stock effect. At pack, scanning a case barcode when fewer units of that SKU were picked warns and does not post the pack until resolved. An authorized user can link an unknown supplier barcode to a SKU and pack quantity at the scan, with the link audited (AUDIT-001) and unique under CAT-001.

**Acceptance evidence:** Import a SKU with a UPC-A unit barcode, an ITF-14 4-unit case barcode and a supplier code. On a 10-unit PO line, scan the case barcode once and the unit UPC twice: received shows 6 and remaining 4, in base units; a third case scan asks for over-receipt authority instead of posting. Scan a GS1-128 carrying GTIN, lot and expiry: the receipt’s lot and expiry equal the barcode’s, and an expired-lot barcode is refused. Receive a pallet by its SSCC against an 856 ASN in one confirmed scan. At pick, a unit UPC scan counts one unit; at pack, scan the case barcode when two units were picked: a warning appears and nothing posts. Run the journey with a USB scanner, a Zebra TC-series handheld and a phone camera. A supervisor links an unknown barcode to a SKU and pack quantity, the link appears in audit, and linking the same code to a second SKU is refused.

### WMS-001 — Mobile receive, put away, pick, pack and count.

Warehouse staff use desktop, handheld and 390px phone screens for scan-driven receiving, directed bin put-away, pick lists, pick verification, packing, transfers and cycle counts. Pickers work one order at a time, a multi-order batch (wave) picked in one bin-sequenced walk with sort-to-order into numbered totes or at the pack station, or zone picks in which each zone picks its part and the parts merge by order at pack. Every picked unit is attributed to its own order’s reservation, so a batch never consumes another order’s allocation, and a short pick in a batch leaves only the affected order short. Picks show source bin, unit, lot, kit components and shortages; the pack step prints the PRINT-001 packing slip and carton labels; separate picked, packed and shipped states prevent a scan or label purchase from pretending goods shipped. Interrupted work resumes its exact task and command. Network loss is visible; queued work reconciles before committing against current availability. A batch is a named wave assigned to a pack station or dock door, and its pick list shows the wave and dock. Each pick line confirms only after its bin and item are scanned (SCAN-002). When a picker reports a pick bin empty or short, Pier proposes the next bin in pick sequence holding sellable, unreserved stock of the same SKU (and lot where tracked); confirming it moves only that line’s reservation to the new bin atomically, directs the picker there and opens a PIER-011 count for the reported bin. No other order’s reservation is taken, and with no such bin the line is picked short. At the returns bench, scanning a returned unit or its return label opens its PIER-010 return line; an inspector the owner has authorized to approve restock records inspection, approval and restock to a scanned bin in one scan, or a write-off with a COST-001 reason code, while a scan by anyone else leaves the unit in quarantine awaiting the separate PIER-010 review.

**Acceptance evidence:** On phone and desktop, receive and put away a partial PO, follow a pick list across bins, scan a wrong SKU, split a pack and shipment, print its packing slip at the pack step, transfer and cycle-count. Batch-pick ten orders in one walk with one line short, sort them to totes and pack each: every order’s shipment consumes only its own reservations and only the short order stays open. Zone-pick one order across two zones and merge it at pack. Interrupt connectivity mid-task and resume; no stale allocation or double movement posts. Release wave 14 to Dock A: the pick list shows the wave and dock, and each line confirms only after its bin and item scans. Report bin B-03 empty on one line: Pier directs the picker to overflow bin B-09, moves only that line’s reservation, opens a count for B-03 and leaves every other order’s reservations unchanged. At the returns bench, an authorized inspector scans a returned sellable unit and a bin and the unit is on hand there after that one scan; scanning a damaged unit with a write-off reason posts the write-off to its mapped account; the same scans by an unauthorized user leave the units in quarantine awaiting review.

### WMS-002 — Directed replenishment and slotting.

Location policies define capacity, pick faces, replenishment thresholds and permitted stock. Pier proposes put-away and reserve-to-pick replenishment, prioritizes tasks and explains ABC slotting recommendations using pick velocity, item size and travel data. Travel data is the walking distance between bins, taken from each location’s aisle, bay and pick-sequence coordinates, together with observed pick-task times. Supervisors approve location changes and physical staff confirm movement; recommendations never relocate inventory on their own.

**Acceptance evidence:** Configure pick-face minimums and bin capacity, receive an oversized item and run a replenishment/ABC plan. An operator completes the approved scan tasks; over-capacity placement is refused and slotting alone changes no stock.

### WMS-003 — Daily dispatch board and carrier cut-offs.

Each warehouse has a dispatch board for the working day in its time zone: the orders due to ship that day (by promise date or the SHIP-001 service’s ship-by date), each carrier’s pickup cut-off time, and counts and lists by state: to pick, picking, picked, packed, labeled and shipped (WMS-001 states; shipped only when PIER-009 dispatch posts). An order that cannot make its carrier’s cut-off from its current state is flagged before the cut-off with its reason (short, unpicked, unpacked or unlabeled). At day close the board records the number of orders and units shipped, the time the last one left and every due order that did not ship with its reason; the record is kept, exportable and feeds the REPORT-002 overdue-orders report.

**Acceptance evidence:** At one warehouse with a 4 pm carrier cut-off, create 20 orders due today and pick, pack, label and ship 18 through public commands, leaving one short and one unpicked. The board’s counts equal the orders’ states throughout, the two at-risk orders are flagged before 4 pm with their reasons, and the day-close record shows 18 orders shipped, the last shipment’s time and the two missed orders with reasons, each equal to an independent count of the day’s dispatches.

### COUNT-001 — Full-warehouse stocktake.

Owners start a wall-to-wall stocktake of a whole location or chosen zones, as accountants require at year-end. Pier captures one movement frontier for every bin in scope as the stocktake snapshot. The owner either freezes the scope, so receipts, picks, transfers and adjustments there are refused with a visible notice while work elsewhere continues, or keeps it live, with each bin checked against its frontier as in PIER-011. Count tasks are assigned by zone on desktop, handheld and phone (WMS-001, SCAN-002); blind counting hides expected quantities, and lots and serials are counted per TRACE-001. Before approval the owner chooses, per zone, what happens to SKU-bin positions nobody counted: leave them unchanged or set them to zero; the preview shows every affected position and its value impact. Variances above owner-set quantity or value thresholds require a recount. Approval posts one set of compensating adjustments at the captured frontier, with reason codes mapped to GL accounts (COST-001) and valuation posted to the ledger once, then lifts the freeze. The completed stocktake is a retained, exportable record of counted, uncounted, variance and value figures.

**Acceptance evidence:** Start a frozen stocktake of one 20-bin warehouse: a pick there is refused with the freeze notice and a pick in the other warehouse succeeds. Blind-count 18 bins by scan and leave 2 uncounted; choose “zero uncounted” for one zone and “leave unchanged” for the other; recount a variance above threshold; approve. Adjustments equal counted minus frontier quantities, uncounted positions follow each zone’s rule, the valuation change posts to QBO once, the freeze lifts and the stocktake record reproduces every figure. Repeat without freezing: a bin that moves after capture becomes stale and must be recounted before approval.

### SALES-001 — Quote, price and convert orders.

Sales staff create versioned quotes, apply customer price lists, volume breaks, discounts and TAX-001 tax estimates and exemptions, send the PRINT-001 quote and order-confirmation documents by email and convert accepted quotes to sales orders without rekeying. Expired pricing and material edits require renewed acceptance. USD totals, units and rounding stay exact and margin/discount permissions are enforced. Staff also import new sales orders from a CSV or spreadsheet file a customer sends: one row per line with customer, PO reference, item by SKU, barcode or customer item code (CAT-001 aliases), quantity and unit, optional price, ship-to and requested date. The preview resolves each row, applies the customer’s price list where no price is given, shows every row error and the SALES-007 warehouse each order would route to, and creates nothing until approved. Approval creates each order once, and re-importing the same file or the same customer PO reference is flagged as a duplicate rather than creating a second order.

**Acceptance evidence:** Quote a case-pack order at two customer tiers, apply an authorized discount, email the quote (its sent PDF kept on the record) and accept one version, then convert once, reserve it and send the order confirmation. A duplicate acceptance cannot create another order; changing price or quantity invalidates earlier approval. Import a 40-line order file for three customers that uses customer item codes, leaves some prices blank and contains one unknown code: the preview prices blank lines from each customer’s price list, names the unknown row and its routed warehouse, and approving creates three orders once; importing the same file again creates none.

### SALES-002 — Customer history, credit and financial returns.

Customer records show contacts, addresses, item references, order and shipment history (including MIGRATE-002 imported history), payment terms, outstanding balances, credit limits and overdue holds. Each customer has an assigned sales rep, either a Pier user or an outside rep record without sign-in. The rep defaults onto the customer’s quotes, orders and invoices and can be changed per document by authorized staff with the change audited; REPORT-002 reports sales and margin by rep. Balances come from SALES-006 invoices and credit notes and PAY-001 payments; Pier produces customer statements and AR aging (current, 1–30, 31–60, 61–90, over 90 days) that reconcile to the ledger. Authorized sales staff review overrides with reasons. Dispatch-linked returns produce partial customer credits/refunds and restock or write-off decisions; physical disposition and financial credit remain separate and reconcile to the ledger.

**Acceptance evidence:** Place an order for a customer over its credit limit, approve an override, partially ship, return damaged and sellable units and issue the correct credit note. Customer balance, AR aging, the emailed statement, quarantine, refund and accounting reconciliation agree without restocking damaged units. Assign an outside rep to the customer: the order and invoice carry that rep, reassigning one order is audited, and REPORT-002 sales and margin by rep credit each invoice to the rep on it.

### SALES-003 — Available-to-promise and fulfillment routing.

Sales staff see present availability and dated available-to-promise by warehouse, accounting for committed demand, inbound confirmations, lead times, transit, safety stock and split deliveries. Which warehouse each new order reserves at, and what happens when that warehouse is short, is decided by the owner’s SALES-007 routing rules; this clause covers what staff do with orders after routing. For an order already routed, staff compare the routed outcome with split or later-shipment options from other eligible warehouses, showing each option’s ship date and carrier cost from SHIP-001. Reprioritizing reservations or changing a customer promise requires authorized review and a customer communication; uncertain inbound supply is labeled rather than guaranteed. When stock arrives, Pier lists the backorders it can now fill, ranked by an owner-set priority rule (promise date, customer priority, order date), and proposes allocations from that receipt. An authorized user releases them in one action, or an AUTO-002 policy releases them within its bounds, without re-authorizing each order. Each backordered line shows an expected fill date: the confirmed date of the earliest inbound supply (PO line or transfer) to its routed warehouse not already allocated to higher-priority backorders under the priority rule, or, with no confirmed supply, today plus the default supplier’s lead time, labeled as an estimate. The date recalculates whenever supply, a confirmation or priority changes, and a changed date flags the order for a customer promise under the review above.

**Acceptance evidence:** Create competing orders with stock in two warehouses and a late inbound PO; compare split and later shipment options, approve one reallocation and changed promise. Another order cannot lose its allocation silently; stale supplier confirmation forces recalculation. Receive the late PO: the fill-backorders list ranks the waiting orders by the priority rule and one release allocates them without re-entering any order. ### SALES-006 — Customer invoices, charge lines and credit notes.

Pier creates the customer invoice. An invoicing policy set per customer or order type decides when: on shipment, once per partial shipment, or before shipment for prepay/proforma. A simple sale has one invoice. A split-shipped order is invoiced once per shipment for exactly the shipped quantities. A prepay order is invoiced on acceptance, and its later shipments reconcile against that invoice without a second charge. Invoices carry freight, handling, surcharge and discount charge lines, tax lines from TAX-001, the customer’s payment terms (due on receipt, Net 15/30/60, end of month, early-payment discount) and a due date derived from those terms and the invoice date. An invoice is drafted, reviewed and authorized. Once authorized it is immutable and is corrected only by a linked credit note or void. Credit notes apply against a named invoice for returns (PIER-010, SALES-002), price adjustments or freight refunds and are refunded through PAY-001 or kept as customer credit. Each authorized invoice and credit note posts once to the connected ledger (ACCOUNT-001/002) and drives the SALES-002 customer balance. Customers priced in CAD or another currency are quoted and invoiced in that currency; the exchange rate is retained and the USD value reported. A paid channel order is one SALES-006 invoice carrying the channel’s charged tax, charge lines and payment under CHANNEL-002, never an unpaid invoice. Voiding an authorized invoice that has no payment applied reverses its ledger posting once, voids any committed TAX-001 transaction, keeps the voided document and reason on the record, and returns its shipped quantities to uninvoiced; an invoice with a payment applied must be credited instead.

**Acceptance evidence:** Ship a Net 30 wholesale order in two partial shipments and invoice each shipment with its own freight line. Invoice a prepay order before shipment, then ship it without a second charge. Due dates follow Net 30 from each invoice date. Issue a credit note against the first invoice for a returned line. Each invoice and credit note posts to QBO exactly once (replay creates no duplicate), and the SALES-002 balance equals invoices less credits and payments. An edit to an authorized invoice is refused; a CAD invoice retains its rate and reports its USD value. Void an unpaid authorized invoice with a reason: its QBO posting and Avalara transaction are reversed once, the voided document stays readable, its shipment is uninvoiced again, and an attempt to void a paid invoice is refused in favor of a credit note.

### SALES-007 — Warehouse routing for incoming orders.

Every order reaches one warehouse before it reserves, as Cin7 Core’s channel locations and Order Routing do. For each order source (each CHANNEL-001 connection, each EDI-001 trading partner, SALES-005 Stall trade orders, SALES-004 recurring orders, SALES-001 manual and file-imported orders and DOC-001 extracted orders) the owner chooses one of three routing modes. **Fixed warehouse** routes every order from that source to one named warehouse. **Channel location** maps each channel-side location (for example each Shopify location) to a Pier warehouse and follows the location the channel assigned. **Rule-based** routes by ship-to zone. A zone is a list of 3-digit or 5-digit ZIP prefixes, a list of US states, or a country. Each zone maps to an ordered eligibility list of warehouses, and the first warehouse on the list is the routed warehouse. The most specific matching zone wins (5-digit ZIP, then 3-digit ZIP, then state, then country, then the source’s default zone). Overlapping zones at the same level are refused when saved, so every address matches exactly one zone. Under rule-based routing, Pier’s rule takes precedence over the fulfillment location a channel assigned; the channel’s location is kept on the order for reference. Warehouses absent from the eligibility list are ineligible for that order. The Amazon FBA location and 3PL warehouses are eligible only for the sources and zones the owner lists them on.

Each source also has a **shortage rule** for when the routed warehouse cannot fill every line:
- **Hold at routed warehouse:** reserve what that warehouse has and backorder the rest there.
- **Next eligible warehouse with full stock:** move the whole order to the first warehouse on the eligibility list that can fill every line. If none can, hold at the routed warehouse.
- **Split by line:** reserve each line at the routed warehouse first and the remainder at the following eligible warehouses in list order, creating one shipment per warehouse. This applies only when the customer’s split-shipment policy allows it; otherwise the order falls back to the next-eligible rule.

The tie-break is always eligibility-list order; Pier never picks a warehouse by any criterion the owner did not configure. Reservation follows PIER-004 at the chosen warehouse’s bins only. No order reserves at an ineligible warehouse. The order shows its routed warehouse and the rule that chose it (source, mode, zone and shortage rule). An order whose address matches no zone, when its source has no default, waits unreserved in the CHANNEL-001 queue with that reason. Backorders wait at the warehouse that holds them for the SALES-003 fill list, and BUY-005 counts them against that warehouse. An authorized user can reroute an unpicked order to another eligible warehouse with a reason: Pier releases the old reservations and creates the new ones atomically, and AUDIT-001 records the change. Rerouting to an ineligible warehouse is refused. A ship-to change before picking reroutes the order under the same rules. Published channel availability follows the same rules (CHANNEL-001): a fixed source publishes its warehouse’s availability; a channel-location source publishes each mapped warehouse to its channel location; a rule-based source publishes the warehouses on its eligibility lists, net of reservations and buffers. Routing rules are versioned and dry-run against recent orders before they take effect.

**Acceptance evidence:** With East and West warehouses and a third FBA location, set Shopify to rule-based routing: one zone covers ZIP prefixes 900–961 and the states WA, OR, NV and AZ and maps to West then East; the default zone maps to East then West. Set Faire to a fixed East warehouse and an EDI 850 partner to rule-based routing. Import a Shopify order to ZIP 94107 and one to ZIP 10001, a Faire order to ZIP 98101 and an EDI 850 to a distribution center in Oregon. The orders reserve at West, East, East and West respectively, each showing the rule that chose it. A 5-unit Shopify order to ZIP 94107 arrives when West has 2 units and East has 10. Under hold-at-routed, 2 reserve at West and 3 backorder at West. Under next-eligible, all 5 reserve at East. Under split-by-line, 2 reserve at West and 3 at East as two shipments; for a no-split customer the order goes to East whole. The FBA stock is never reserved by any of them. An order with no matching zone and no default waits unreserved in the queue. A manual reroute from West to East is audited, and a reroute to the FBA location is refused. Shopify’s published availability equals West plus East net of reservations and buffer, and Faire’s equals East only. A dry run of a changed rule over the last 30 days of orders lists every order whose warehouse would change.

### TAX-001 — US sales tax on quotes, orders and invoices.

Owners configure one sales-tax source of truth per company: Avalara AvaTax, Stripe Tax, QuickBooks Online automated sales tax, or Pier’s owner-maintained rate table by jurisdiction. Tax is calculated per line from the ship-to address, product tax code and each state’s rule, including whether freight and handling are taxable; origin-based states follow the provider or table rule. Quotes and orders show an estimate labeled with its source; the invoice amount is final at authorization. With Avalara or Stripe Tax, the authorized invoice commits the transaction, and a credit note or void adjusts or voids that committed transaction. With QBO automated sales tax, Pier adopts the tax QBO returns on the posted invoice and flags any difference from the estimate. Resale and other exempt customers hold exemption certificates by state, with reason, number, expiry and document (DOC-001), linked to the customer’s Avalara exemptions where connected. A missing or expired certificate for the ship-to state stops the exemption and flags the order for review; Pier neither charges nor exempts silently. Orders imported from a store channel (Stall, Shopify, WooCommerce, BigCommerce, eBay, Etsy, Faire) keep the tax the channel charged the customer, per line and on shipping, as channel-collected tax mapped through the channel’s tax rules (CHANNEL-002); the company’s tax source never recalculates them, and a difference from its estimate is reported rather than applied. Marketplace orders whose marketplace collects and remits the tax record it as facilitator-collected. A provider timeout leaves the invoice pending rather than taxed at an invented rate. Nexus registration and filing remain with the accountant or tax provider.

**Acceptance evidence:** For each supported tax source, quote and invoice one taxable and one resale-exempt order to two US states, one that taxes freight and one that does not. Line and freight tax follow each state’s rule and the exempt order cites its certificate; expiring that certificate flags the next order. The authorized invoice’s tax equals the provider’s committed transaction and the QBO-posted invoice; a credit note adjusts the committed amount once; a provider timeout leaves the invoice pending with no invented tax. ### BUY-005 — Reorder-point list and suggested quantities.

Most weeks a buyer replenishes from the reorder list, as Cin7 Core’s low-stock reorder does. Parameters are kept per SKU and location: the reorder point (minimum before reorder), safety stock, and a replenishment method, either a **fixed reorder quantity** or an **order-up-to maximum**. A SKU-level default fills any location without its own values. Each supplier-SKU term (PIER-002) adds the supplier’s case pack, minimum order quantity, optional minimum order value, lead time and an optional supplier reorder quantity. Each SKU has one default supplier, with an optional per-location override.

**Trigger.** An owner setting chooses per-location or company-wide replenishment. Per location (the default), a SKU-location is listed when its position is at or below that location’s reorder point. Company-wide, a SKU is listed when its total position across the warehouses included in planning is at or below its company reorder point (by default the sum of the location points), and the PO goes to the owner’s default receiving warehouse.

**Position.** The position nets everything that will change the location’s stock: sellable on-hand, less reservations, less unfilled backorders routed to that location (SALES-007), plus unreceived quantities on committed PO lines destined there, plus inbound transfers in transit or committed to it, less committed outbound transfers. Quarantine stock and unconfirmed forecasts never count. Kit demand counts against the kit’s components (KIT-001); a kit without pre-assembled stock is not itself listed.

**Suggested quantity.** With a fixed reorder quantity, the suggestion is reorder quantity + reorder point − position, using the first reorder quantity found in this order: the default supplier’s, then the SKU-location’s, then the SKU default. With an order-up-to maximum, the suggestion is maximum − position. It is rounded up to whole supplier case packs. If it is still below the supplier’s minimum order quantity, it is raised to that minimum and rounded up to the case pack again. A supplier minimum order value is checked per PO draft and shown as unmet; it is never padded silently.

**Reorder list.** Each line shows the SKU, location, supplier, lead time and every term of the calculation: on-hand, reserved, backorders, on order, inbound transfers, position, reorder point, method, unrounded need, rounding applied and the final quantity. When sales history exists, the line also shows BUY-001 days of cover and the projected stockout date. The buyer ticks lines and may edit a quantity or switch to another supplier; a supplier switch recalculates rounding with that supplier’s terms. One action creates one PO draft per supplier and destination warehouse. The drafts go through PIER-005 and BUY-002 approval, and on-hand stock does not change. A SKU-location already covered by an uncommitted PO draft or approved PO stays on the list, marked with that document, and its remaining suggestion is the suggested quantity less the quantity on that document, never below zero. A rerun therefore suggests nothing twice, and deleting a draft restores the full suggestion. A line leaves the list when committed supply or a receipt lifts its position above the reorder point. This trigger and position are the single definition used by the AUTO-001 low-stock check and the REPORT-002 low-stock report, so all three list the same SKU-locations. AUTO-002 may create the drafts on a schedule within a bounded policy; it never approves them.

**Acceptance evidence:** Build a seeded scenario with East and West warehouses through public commands. SKU A at East has reorder point 20, fixed reorder quantity 40, 8 on hand, an open PO line for 6 and supplier S1 (case pack 12, minimum 24): the list suggests 48. SKU B at West has reorder point 10, order-up-to maximum 60, 30 on hand of which 25 are reserved, and a 7-unit unfilled backorder; its default supplier is S2 (case pack 6), with S3 as an alternative: the list suggests 66 from S2. SKU C at East has reorder point 5, 4 on hand, a location reorder quantity of 8 and an S1 supplier reorder quantity of 3 (case pack 1, minimum 10): the supplier quantity applies and the minimum lifts the suggestion to 10. SKU D at West has reorder point 10 and 6 on hand, with 8 in transit from East: it is not listed. SKU E has 2 on hand at East (reorder point 5, reorder quantity 10, supplier S1) and 40 at West: per location it is listed at East for 13. The list shows exactly A, B, C and E with those quantities and every calculation term. One action creates exactly two drafts: S1 to East with A 48, C 10 and E 13, and S2 to West with B 66. A rerun shows the same four lines, each marked with its draft and a remaining suggestion of zero. Deleting the S2 draft restores B’s suggestion of 66 and nothing else; committing B’s PO removes B from the list. Switching to company-wide replenishment removes E, because West covers it. The AUTO-001 low-stock exceptions and the REPORT-002 low-stock report list the same SKU-locations as the reorder list in each setting. An independent calculator in the test computes every expected quantity from the exported stock, orders, POs, transfers and terms.

### REPORT-002 — Report definitions, as-of dates and independent reconciliation.

Each REPORT-001 report states its definition in the report and computes to it:

- **Stock valuation:** quantity times the remaining cost of each open COST-001 layer (FIFO) or the moving-average unit cost, by SKU, location and lot; the total equals the COST-001 inventory value and the ledger inventory-asset balance at the same date, with any difference listed.
- **Movement/lot history:** every movement with its source document, actor, lot/serial and before/after quantity.
- **Warehouse availability:** per location, on-hand, committed, available (on-hand sellable less reservations), inbound (open PO lines with their confirmed date), transit and quarantine.
- **Sales:** net sales from SALES-006 invoices less credit notes, and units shipped, by customer, channel, SKU, sales rep (SALES-002) and period.
- **Purchasing:** ordered, received, billed and open quantities and values by supplier and SKU, with BUY-004 on-time and fill rates.
- **Gross margin:** net sales less COGS from the cost layers actually consumed (including landed cost and kit components), per invoice line, by customer, channel, SKU and sales rep; totals equal ledger revenue less COGS.
- **Inventory aging:** on-hand quantity and value of each cost layer by that layer’s receipt date (a pre-assembled kit by its assembly date; a transfer keeps the original receipt date), in owner-set buckets (default 0–30, 31–60, 61–90, 91–180 and over 180 days; any intervals from 1 to over 360 days), shown by quantity and by value.
- **Dead stock:** SKU-locations with stock on hand and no sale shipment, kit consumption or transfer out within an owner-set number of days (default 180), with value and last-movement date.
- **Low stock:** exactly the SKU-locations (or SKUs, under company-wide replenishment) that the BUY-005 trigger lists, with the same position terms and reorder point. Available stock without inbound supply is shown alongside, with safety stock, and stock below safety stock is flagged critical. The report, the BUY-005 reorder list and the AUTO-001 low-stock check never disagree on which SKUs are low.
- **Turnover:** COGS for the selected period divided by average inventory value over that period (the mean of daily closing values), with days of inventory equal to period days divided by turnover.
- **Overdue orders:** sales order lines not shipped by their customer promise date (the requested date when no promise was given) and PO lines not received by their supplier-confirmed date (the expected date when unconfirmed), measured in each location’s time zone.

Sales, gross margin, dead stock, turnover and purchasing include MIGRATE-002 imported history and CHANNEL-001 backfill for periods before cutover, so a SKU sold shortly before cutover is not dead stock on cutover day. A period that spans cutover combines imported and native figures, and the report labels each figure’s source. Turnover for pre-cutover periods averages the imported month-end inventory values, a method the report states, rather than daily closing values. Every report runs as of a past date, reconstructing quantities, cost layers and values from movements and layers at the end of that day in the location’s time zone. Valuation and aging as of a closed accounting period equal the figures captured at its close. Owner-set parameters (buckets, thresholds, period, inbound treatment) are saved with the report and shown on its output and export.

**Acceptance evidence:** Build a seeded scenario through public commands: two warehouses, FIFO layers received 10, 45, 100 and 200 days ago, a kit assembled 20 days ago, a transfer, sales and a return, landed cost, a write-off, a late PO and a late customer promise. Run every named report on it and on PIER-SCALE-1. An independent calculator in the test computes the expected figures from the same exported movements and cost layers, and every report figure matches it exactly. Aging places each layer by its receipt date (transferred stock keeps its original date, the kit uses its assembly date), and changing the buckets to 30/90/360 re-buckets the same layers. Dead stock with a 90-day threshold lists exactly the expected SKUs. Low stock lists exactly the SKU-locations of the BUY-005 reorder list in the same scenario. Sales and margin by rep credit each invoice to its rep. Turnover equals COGS over average inventory. Overdue lists the late PO by its confirmed date and the late order by its promise date. Valuation, aging-by-value and margin totals equal COST-001 and the QBO inventory-asset, COGS and revenue balances. Valuation and aging run as of a past month-end equal the figures captured at that date.

### CHANNEL-001 — Connected channel orders and stock.

Pier connects [Stall](https://github.com/just-every/run-stall), Faire, Shopify, WooCommerce, BigCommerce, Amazon, eBay and Etsy for item/variant/kit mapping, orders, cancellations, refunds, fulfillment status and stock publication. External IDs link source records to Pier; webhook intake and scheduled reconciliation handle duplicates, out-of-order changes and missed events. Each imported order routes to one warehouse under its channel’s SALES-007 rule and reserves there. Channel buffers and the same SALES-007 rules decide which warehouses’ sellable availability each channel publishes, after reservations, never raw physical stock. On connection, each store channel backfills at least 12 months of past orders (longer when the owner asks and the channel’s API provides it) as MIGRATE-002 history. Backfilled orders are deduplicated against imported incumbent history by channel order ID, and they create no reservation, invoice, ledger posting, tax transaction or customer message. Orders still open at connection import as live orders after review. Unmapped SKUs, lag and oversell risk enter a visible queue with an owner and repair action. Faire wholesale orders are accepted or declined within Faire’s window, lines can be backordered with a ship date, shipments and tracking return to Faire, retailer cancellations release reservations, and Faire payouts reconcile net of commission to the SALES-006 invoice and the ledger. Per channel, owners choose manual fulfillment or automatic pick, pack and ship when the channel, a 3PL or another system reports the shipment; automatic fulfillment dispatches the reservations once, on that shipment evidence. Amazon FBA inventory is its own Pier location: inbound shipment plans move stock into it through transit, FBA inventory reports reconcile it like a THIRDPL-001 snapshot without overwriting the ledger, FBA orders consume it, and FBA fees and reimbursements reconcile through the CHANNEL-002 settlement. How each channel order’s tax, payment, refunds and payouts reach the ledger is CHANNEL-002. Stock publication follows each change: after a movement or reservation changes a SKU’s published availability, Pier sends the new quantity to every connected channel and has it accepted by the channel’s API within 60 seconds at the 95th percentile, within each channel’s rate limits; an update not accepted within that time shows as lag in the queue (SYNC-001), and the channel’s own processing time after acceptance is reported per channel, not hidden. An approved Amazon FBA inbound plan holds its units at the source warehouse from approval until they leave for transit: held units are available to no channel, and each channel’s availability view shows the hold and its plan (for example “100 held for FBA transfer”). On connection, including reconnection after a MIGRATE-001 cutover, Pier links each existing channel listing to its SKU by the listing or variant ID carried from the incumbent, then by SKU or barcode, without creating, relisting or changing any listing; a listing it cannot match waits in the queue awaiting SKU match and publishes no quantity until mapped, and mapping it links it once.

### CHANNEL-002 — Channel order money: tax as charged, payments, clearing accounts and payouts.

Every order from Stall, Faire, Shopify, WooCommerce, BigCommerce, Amazon, eBay and Etsy reaches the ledger as the money the channel actually took, as Cin7 Core does with its tax-rule and payment mappings. An imported paid order becomes one SALES-006 invoice whose tax lines carry the tax the channel charged, per line and on shipping, to the cent and labeled channel-collected; TAX-001 does not recalculate it. Shipping and discounts are charge lines, with order-level discounts allocated to lines for margin. The channel payment applies to that invoice through its mapped clearing account, so the invoice posts once to QBO or Xero as paid, and SALES-002 balances and AR aging never count it as owed. Orders the channel has not yet captured (Faire net terms, pending payment, authorized but not captured) stay open invoices until the channel reports capture. A channel edit before authorization revises the draft invoice; after authorization it produces a credit note or an additional invoice. A full or partial refund on the channel produces one SALES-006 credit note for the refunded lines, shipping and tax and one refund out of the same clearing account; physical restock follows PIER-010 inspection. Each channel payout or settlement (Shopify Payments payouts, Amazon settlement reports including FBA fees, eBay and Etsy payouts, Faire payouts, Stripe payouts for Stall) imports with its orders, refunds, fees, chargebacks, reserves and adjustments: fees post to their mapped expense accounts, the net deposit posts to the bank account and matches the bank feed in the ledger, and the clearing account returns to zero for that payout, with any residue an assigned reconciliation case. ## Push further: inventory agents that finish the job

Agents act on authorized real business evidence under explicit human control. Model output is never physical stock, financial settlement or a substitute for provider acknowledgment. Each action uses ordinary versioned commands, and execution stops visibly when required authority or evidence is missing.

### AGENT-001 — Evidence-backed preparation and human authority.

Agent access is a separately scoped role: it reads allowed sales, supplier, stock, count and import evidence and proposes replenishment, reallocation, count-drift or migration work with assumptions and exact input versions. Current humans approve each stock/purchase consequence through the same native commands. A model never creates on-hand stock, invents physical counts or self-approves; forecasts and proposals remain separate from actual inventory.

**Acceptance evidence:** As an agent, prepare each of the four proposal kinds and attempt stock writing, approval and stale-version application. Refusals leave business state unchanged; the appropriate buyer/operator/warehouse/owner can review evidence and execute only an exact current approved consequence.

### AGENT-002 — Optimize replenishment against cash and service.

The inventory agent forecasts from real channel sell-through, seasonality, promotions and stockout-censored demand, with uncertainty and backtesting. It combines MOQ/case packs, supplier reliability, confirmed receipts, actual margin and owner cash/service limits to compare executable PO/transfer options and draft the selected POs. An authorized buyer approves exact spend/terms and recipient before the agent sends through BUY-002. Policy/budget changes or new demand invalidate stale approval; the agent verifies arrival and accounting rather than stopping at draft creation.

**Acceptance evidence:** Provide connected sell-through with a censored stockout and unreliable supplier, compare a cash-constrained and service-maximizing plan, approve one PO/send and change a material input before a second. The second blocks; the first is followed through actual receipt, landed cost and reconciled ledger with forecast-versus-actual explanation.

### AGENT-003 — Close supplier confirmations and customer promises.

The agent reads approved POs, authorized Gmail/Microsoft 365 supplier correspondence, ASN/EDI events, actual receipt history and customer commitments. It chases missing confirmations, extracts changed dates/quantities with source citations, proposes reallocation/substitutes and re-promises affected orders, then follows receipts, shipments and financial reconciliation to closure. Buyers approve supplier sends, additional spend and changed customer promises; delegated message policies name recipient/content bounds and revoke immediately. An acknowledgment alone cannot close physical or ledger work.

**Acceptance evidence:** Leave a supplier confirmation overdue, authorize a chase, ingest a changed ASN and compare affected customer promises. Approve one change and decline another; only approved messages/actions occur. A partial late receipt keeps the case open until the remaining supply/customer outcome and ledger reconcile or a human authorizes cancellation.

### AGENT-004 — Repair channel oversell causes.

The agent correlates channel webhook/cursor history, mappings, reservations, 3PL snapshots and ledger differences to explain an oversell’s actual cause. It proposes connector repairs, substitutes, transfers and customer options, then carries approved corrections through channel, warehouse and accounting queues and verifies the next reconciliation. Operators approve cancellations, refunds, physical adjustments and promise changes; the agent cannot manufacture stock or treat an uncertain snapshot as a physical count.

**Acceptance evidence:** Create a missed channel event and stale 3PL snapshot that overstates availability. The agent cites the conflicting records, proposes repair and customer options and executes only approved changes. A denied refund is not sent; closure requires corrected channel availability, real fulfillment/count evidence and reconciled accounting.

### AGENT-005 — Prove cutover and reconciliation.

The agent maps incumbent exports and source artifacts to items, terms, opening stock/valuation and open documents, identifies duplicates and explains differences by unit, bin, source row and ledger account. It prepares a cutoff/delta plan and checks warehouse, customer/supplier and financial totals before and after cutover. Owners approve mappings, merges and unresolved balance treatments; source artifacts and corrections remain traceable and the agent cannot silently discard unsupported fields.

**Acceptance evidence:** Use a named-source cutover with a duplicate SKU, unit conflict, open backorder and accounting difference. Inspect source-cited proposed mappings and totals, reject a merge, approve corrected mappings and cut over once. Replay and final-delta reconciliation show no duplicate opening or unaccounted field/value.

### AGENT-006 — Live questions and reviewed bulk work.

People ask operational questions against permission-scoped live records: why stock is short, what is late, which orders lose margin and what changing supplier/date/allocation would do. Answers cite records, quantities, data freshness and uncertainty. Bulk proposals preview exact changed rows, cash/service impact and affected promises, enforce per-row authority and approval, and become ordinary commands; stale versions block the affected plan rather than improvising a fallback.

**Acceptance evidence:** Ask a restricted warehouse user why an order is short and propose bulk promise/PO changes. Answers cite allowed evidence without exposing forbidden costs; preview, edit and approve the plan, then mutate an input. The stale action refuses and the remaining authorized results retain ordinary command/audit links.
